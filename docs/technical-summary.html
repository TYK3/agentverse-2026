AGENTVERSE 2026  /  ROBOTICS TRACK  /  TECHNICAL SUMMARY

AFTERSHOCK

A two-robot search-and-rescue agent that maps a collapsed building, predicts what may fail next, and sends help only along routes it has checked.
Every move traces to a reading; a scene change revokes the route; physics and a human, not a language model, authorise movement.
SIMULATION · NOT A FIELD SYSTEM

Problem

After an earthquake, rescuers cannot see inside a collapsed building, and an aftershock can bring down more of it on the people who go in. Sending a person or a heavy machine in blind wastes the first hours and adds risk.

Time is the constraint: “the vast majority of survivors are brought out within 24 hours by local teams”, and more than 90% within three days (Ilan Kelman, UCL disaster researcher, to AFP, 9 Feb 2023¹). A robot that maps safely in those first hours saves the time a blind entry loses.

Users

  • USAR team lead / operator (primary): decides where it is safe to send people or machines; approves risky moves. Often hands busy, so every command works by voice or typed.
  • Trapped casualty: no screen and no login. Hears the Scout call out, answers by tapping or speech, gets supplies and spoken guidance in their language.
  • Next team in: reads the handover report: what is safe, who is where, what was approved.

What it does

  • Scout explores and maps: cells are seen, inferred or unknown; only seen-free cells can carry a route.
  • Finds casualties from pose, thermal and sound (no faces); predicts stable / at-risk / collapsed blocks.
  • Responder delivers water, a first aid kit or a radio, clears light debris with a 0.5 kg-payload arm.
  • Voice: operator commands, casualty translation, cited first-aid steps. Handover report at the end.

How it works

MuJoCo sensors (10 Hz) → perception map (2 Hz) → local A* route → MuJoCo physics check → move permit → human gate if risky → Responder action → log/decision

Perceive. Scout ray-cast LiDAR (360 rays), depth/RGB, a 16×12 thermal grid and acoustic bearing build an occupancy map and a fused casualty estimate with a confidence.

Decide. Local A* proposes a route over seen cells, keeping 0.15 m from walls. A confidence gate picks act, re-look (send the Scout back) or refer to the operator; it never guesses silently.

Check. MuJoCo simulates an aftershock against the route; a route through a block that would move is rejected and re-planned. Passing earns a move permit.

Adapt. Any sensed change (a block lands, debris moves) revokes the permit; the Responder stops, the Scout re-scans, and a new route is verified and explained in one line.

Critical failure, handled on screen

Stale map: debris moves after a route was approved. The LiDAR scan diff revokes the permit, the Responder stops on the spot, and travel resumes only after every route cell is freshly re-scanned and physics-checked again.

Measured (B1 run, seed 3): block lands at 50.1 s → permit revoked 50.1 s → re-granted after a fresh check 52.3 s → Responder moving on a new route 53.1 s (sim time).

Device = the demo laptop: CPU only, 8 GB RAM, no GPU (WSL2, 12 threads). Target robot compute: Jetson Orin NX 16 GB, so the 4.1 GB planner fits beside mapping.

Stack

  • Sim: MuJoCo 4.0.0, Python 3.12; scenes: backup, building, tower, multi-level (L1–B3).
  • Lanes: sim, perception, planner, voice: async Python on one validated topic bus (FastAPI + uvicorn, WebSocket /ws); optional sim-in-own-process mode.
  • Console: TypeScript 5.9, Three.js 0.181, Vite 7; browser speech for voice; Playwright tests.
  • Local LLM: Ollama + Gemma 4 E2B. Cloud: Google GenAI SDK.

States and interfaces

  • Map cell: seen / inferred / unknown; hazard: stable / at-risk / collapsed.
  • Responder: idle, moving, arrived, blocked, frozen (shaking).
  • Gate: act, re-look, refer. Permit: granted, revoked.
  • Lanes talk only through typed bus topics (e.g. route/proposed → route/verified → permit/route); the browser may write only operator/command and gate/answer.

Safety rules and limits

  • Risky moves (near a casualty, into an at-risk cell) and any emergency call need explicit operator approval.
  • A model answer never authorises movement; physics and the permit do.
  • No faces, no diagnosis: casualty vitals are simulated and labelled; first aid is cited guidance for a human.
  • Every decision is logged with its perception, model, physics result and outcome.
  • Limits: simulation only; hazard and casualty outputs are estimates, not engineering or medical advice; sim robots are scaled down (a real Jackal needs ~0.8 m doorways); real use needs independent validation.

Evidence: what we measured

  • End-to-end runner (one command, auto-approve, local models only): B1 seeds 3, 5, 18 and 19 pass 17/17 checks.
  • Multi-level descent to B2: seeds 0–4 pass 9/9: the Scout descends, maps a dropped slab, stops, re-routes, reaches the casualty.
  • Test suites: about 900 Python tests and 36 browser tests on the main branch.
  • Failures we found and fixed: the most common miss was “supply placed within 0.5 m of the casualty” (the collision ray read the casualty as a wall; re-plans started from a stale cell). After both fixes, seeds 3 and 5 pass 17/17.

Hardware concept (not built)

Scout: AgileX LIMO Pro, Jetson Orin Nano, 2D LiDAR (12 m), depth, FLIR Lepton 3.5, 4-mic array, IMU; ~2.3 h.

Responder: Clearpath Jackal, Jetson Orin NX 16 GB, Livox Mid-360 3D LiDAR, OAK-D Lite, Lepton 3.5, mics + speaker, Kinova Gen3 lite arm; ~26 kg, ~2 h.

Track fit

Perception-driven action (25%): each log line names the reading behind it; a re-look is a decision, not a fallback.

Adaptation (25%): an aftershock or a block dropped live from the console revokes the route; the new one is drawn with its reason.

Autonomy (15%): core decisions run on the device within a stated, metered budget; cloud models are advisory.

Impact and quality (35%): the stale-map failure is handled on screen; the video is one take with one unscripted change.
